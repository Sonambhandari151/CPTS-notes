- **TCP-SYN scan(-sS)**: Default . Just send **SYN** , never complete three-way handshake.
- If our target sends a `SYN-ACK` flagged packet back to us, Nmap detects that the port is `open`.
- If the target responds with an `RST` flagged packet, it is an indicator that the port is `closed`.
- If Nmap does not receive a packet back, it will display it as `filtered`. Depending on the firewall configuration, certain packets may be dropped or ignored by the firewall.

### Nmap Host Discovery (Short Notes)

- **`-sn`** → Ping scan only, disables port scanning.
    
- **Default behavior**:
    
    - On LAN → sends **ARP requests** first.
        
    - Remote → uses **ICMP Echo Requests**.
        
- **`-PE`** → Force ICMP Echo Requests (“ping”).
    
- **`--disable-arp-ping`** → Stop ARP, force ICMP even on LAN.
    
- **`--packet-trace`** → Shows all packets sent/received.
    
- **`--reason`** → Explains why a host is marked up (ARP reply, ICMP reply, etc.).
    
- **Host detection flow**:
    
    1. Send ARP (LAN) / ICMP (remote)
        
    2. Receive reply → host is up
        
    3. Report latency & MAC address (if ARP used)

### 🔑 Nmap Port States (in easy words)

1. **open**
    
    - The port is active and accepting connections.
        
    - Example: If port **80** is open, you can connect to a web server.
        

---

2. **closed**
    
    - The port is reachable, but nothing is listening on it.
        
    - Example: Knocking on a door and someone says “this door isn’t in use” (RST flag).
        

---

3. **filtered**
    
    - Nmap can’t tell if it’s open or closed because something (like a firewall) is blocking the reply.
        
    - Example: You knock on the door, but you don’t get any answer.
        

---

4. **unfiltered**
    
    - The port is reachable, but Nmap cannot figure out if it’s open or closed (only seen in TCP-ACK scans).
        
    - Example: You know the door exists, but you can’t tell if it’s locked or unlocked.
        

---

5. **open|filtered**
    
    - No response at all. Could mean the port is open **or** a firewall is silently dropping packets.
        
    - Example: You knock on the door, but you can’t tell if someone quietly ignores you (firewall) or if the door is open but no one answers.
        

---

6. **closed|filtered**
    
    - Only happens in **idle scans**. Nmap cannot tell if the port is closed **or** filtered by a firewall.
        
    - Example: You try a backdoor route, but you can’t figure out if the door is locked or blocked by a guard.
        

---

👉 In short:

- **open = active service**
    
- **closed = no service, but reachable**
    
- **filtered = blocked by firewall**
    
- **unfiltered = accessible, but unknown**
    
- **open|filtered = no response at all**
    
- **closed|filtered = can’t decide between closed or blocked**
